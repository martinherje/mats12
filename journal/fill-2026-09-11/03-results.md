The null repeats the whole procedure, layer and C re-chosen each time, on labels shuffled within each topic and stratum cell, 100 times (`PE * perm_null`). Identical to `RESULTS`. The interval is a topic-block bootstrap on accuracy (2000 resamples of the 15 test topics); no interval on AUROC was computed or stored. The word-count column is the AUROC of sentence length on the same 30 rows for the label as the file stores it, legal for the illegality rows and harmful for the harm rows: 0.60 means longer sentences read as legal, 0.79 means the same but stronger, 0.33 means shorter sentences read as harmful. It is the same in both conditions because the test rows are the same. On the illegality harmless → harmful rows word count alone (0.79) beats the sentence-only probe (0.71): illegal-harmful rows average 11.2 words against 13.9 for legal-harmful in the design set (`data/scenarios.csv`), an imbalance I did not correct, so that row is not evidence on its own.

Reading the rows, with positive meaning called illegal (or called harmful for the harm probes):

Sentence only, illegality trained on harmful rows and tested on harmless rows. The probe learned to tell illegal-harmful from legal-harmful sentences, where harm is constant, and it ranks illegal-harmless above legal-harmless sentences on 15 unseen topics at 0.74, above all 100 shuffled runs. At its own cut-off it calls 12 of the 30 illegal and gets 21 right: 9 of the 15 illegal rows and 12 of the 15 legal rows (`FOLLOWUP` recompute, my refit). Whatever it carried across is not harm, because harm did not vary in training. The nine it got wrong are in the next section.

Sentence only, the reverse. Trained on harmless rows, it ranks the harmful rows at 0.71, above 97 of 100 shuffled runs and only just above the null's 95th percentile (0.69), and word count alone does better on the same rows (0.79). I do not use this number as evidence.

Sentence only, harm trained on illegal rows, tested on legal rows: 0.95, 100 of 100. Harm trained on legal rows, tested on illegal rows: 0.83, 100 of 100, at layer 3. Harm carries across legality with room to spare, in both directions, and word count points the other way there (0.33: the shortest rows are the illegal-harmful ones), so length is not doing it.

Sentence + question, illegality trained on harmful rows and tested on harmless rows. The ranking is again 0.74 above 100 of 100 shuffled runs (the two 0.74s are 167 of 225 rank pairs in both conditions, a coincidence, not the same probe), but the cut-off does not carry: the probe calls every one of the 30 harmless test rows legal, accuracy 50% with a 50–50 interval, beating only 36 of 100 shuffled runs on accuracy, while the same probe is perfect on the held-out harmful rows of the same topics (`test_in_acc` 1.0, `PE lp_4b_prompted L_h2nh`). AUROC and accuracy diverge here because AUROC scores only the order of the 30 test rows against each other and never touches the cut-off, so it is unmoved by anything that shifts all 30 rows together; accuracy uses the intercept fitted among the harmful training rows, and at the answer position the whole harmless stratum sits on the legal side of that intercept (every illegal-harmless test row scores between −0.96 and −3.85, `FOLLOWUP`), so all 30 are called legal. That is the same fact the cosine reports at this position: under the question the illegality direction has taken on harm, so taking harm away moves every score toward legal by more than the legality signal is worth.

Sentence + question, the reverse: 0.91, 100 of 100, accuracy 70%. Word count reaches 0.79 on these rows, below the probe but not far below, and at this layer the plain mean-difference direction from the same training rows scores the same test rows at 0.41 where the logistic probe scores 0.91 (`PE lp_4b_prompted L_nh2h`, `factorial.massmean_half_contrast_auroc_cross_stratum_test`; in the other seven tests the two agree within about 0.1). I have not looked at why, and I do not read the 0.91 as a shared illegality direction.

Sentence + question, the harm probes: 0.97 and 0.99 on AUROC, 100 of 100 both, with accuracies of 63% (beating 87 of 100) and 87% (100 of 100). The illegal → legal harm probe's cut-off drifts under the question too, less badly than the illegality one.

Results - the two directions

At the layer each headline probe chose, the factorial illegality and harm directions (mean differences over the 30 training topics, each averaged across the two strata of the other concept) sit at cos(d_illegal, d_harm) = +0.09 in the sentence-only condition against a label-swap band of −0.28 to +0.26, and at +0.70 under the question against a band of −0.54 to +0.49 (`PE lp_4b L_h2nh` and `PE lp_4b_prompted L_h2nh`, `factorial.cos_dlegal_dharm` negated; band = the negated `cosine_curve.null_hi` / `null_lo` at that layer, 50 label swaps). Over all 33 layers the sentence-only curve is outside its band at one layer (layer 9) and runs +0.09 to +0.19 over layers 17–32, consistently positive but inside a band whose top runs +0.19 to +0.35. Under the question the curve leaves the band at layer 14 and stays outside through layer 32, running +0.85 to +0.95 over layers 17–32; but the band's upper edge there runs +0.72 to +0.92, so at the answer position a label-swapped within-cell contrast also lines up with harm, and what carries the claim is the sign at 19 consecutive layers and the probe-layer figure, not the margin over the band (`RESULTS` Directions; computed from the `cosine_curve` arrays).

With the harm direction projected out (k = 1, orthogonal to the between-stratum harm contrast), the illegality direction's AUROC for illegal within the harmless test rows goes from 0.68 to 0.68 in the sentence-only condition and from 0.85 to 0.74 under the question (`factorial.dlegal_auroc_within_harmless` and `dlegal_minus_harm_top1_auroc_within_harmless`). The illegality direction predicts harm on the test rows at 0.50 in the sentence-only condition and 0.75 under the question (1 − `factorial.dlegal_auroc_predicting_harm_test`). These are directions built from all four training quadrants, not the conditional probe, so they are not generalisation numbers.

Results - checks that were never trained on

The sentence-only headline probe calls 33 of the 60 plain acts ("You cook pasta") legal and 61 of 61 negations legal; under the question the headline probe calls 58 of 60 and 58 of 61 legal (`extra_sets.simple` and `extra_sets.negated`, `frac_predicted_legal_1` × n, in the two `L_h2nh` files). The reverse illegality probes: 56 of 60 and 55 of 61 (sentence only), 59 of 60 and 36 of 61 (under the question). The harm probes call between 0% and 12% of the check rows harmful (`frac_predicted_harmful_1`, four `H_*` files). The 27 plain acts the sentence-only headline probe calls illegal is the worst number in the run; the plain acts average 7.7 words against 11 to 14 for the design rows, and I have not tested whether length explains it. The negation check carries little: under the sentence-only headline probe the negations score −7.90 on average (my refit; matches the team's −7.9), far past any legal design row, so "not flagged" was never in doubt.

Three of the 244 design rows contain a legality word ("disorderly conduct", "medical license", "criminalises"). With them dropped, the sentence-only harmful → harmless illegality probe scores 0.74, 100 of 100 on AUROC, at layer 24 (`PE lp_4b L_h2nh_nocue`, `n_rows` 241).

Results - just asking the model

The fair baseline is the model's own Yes−No logit for "Under the law of the United States, is the following action illegal? Answer with exactly one word: Yes or No." scored on exactly the 30 harmless-stratum rows the headline probe is tested on. It sorts illegal from legal at 0.81 (0.809 from `RAW` with the seed-0 test topics; `RESULTS` last line; `scripts/common.py just_ask_auroc`); the probe scores 0.74 in both conditions. On the 30 harmful-stratum rows of the reverse test the model's logit reaches 1.00 against the probes' 0.71 and 0.91. By the rule I set before the run, the probe does not beat asking. On the one-word answer the model is right on 19 of those 30 rows (the sentence-only probe on 21) and answers "No" to 10 of the 15 illegal-harmless test rows (`RAW`, computed tonight; this settles the 9-or-10 question in the log at 10).

Over all 244 design rows the one-word answer is right on 76%: 97% of illegal-harmful, 34% of illegal-harmless, 75% of legal-harmful, 97% of legal-harmless rows, 55% on the two off-diagonal quadrants together, no refusals or unparsed answers (`ASK`, `accuracy`, `per_quadrant`, `offdiagonal_accuracy`, `refused_or_unparsed_frac`). The mean Yes−No logit by quadrant is +3.82, −0.98, −0.99, −3.46, and the model says "illegal" on 97%, 34%, 25% and 3% of the rows (`RAW`, set = main, exclude = 0; recomputed tonight, matches `journal/writeup-skeleton.md`). An illegal harmless act and a legal harmful act land at the same place on the answer axis. Within each stratum the graded logit still ranks illegal above legal, at 0.83 among harmless rows and 0.95 among harmful rows (`RAW`, computed tonight). The pooled off-diagonal logit AUROC of 0.49 in `ASK` (`logit_auroc_offdiagonal`) is not the fair comparison: pooled over both strata it asks the logit to sort illegal-harmless from legal-harmful, which is scored across the harm confound the design removes.

## Figure 1 caption (`figures/fig1_lp_4b.png`; panels as drawn by `scripts/figures.py`)

Figure 1. A: the four cross tests in both conditions. Each marker is the AUROC of a probe trained inside one stratum of the other concept (the label under the axis reads trained → tested) and scored on the other stratum of the 15 test topics it never saw, 30 sentences per test; black circles are the sentence-only condition, blue squares the sentence + question condition (the legend in the figure calls it "sentence, then the question 'is this illegal?'"). The grey bar behind each marker runs from 0.5 to the 95th percentile of 100 runs of the same procedure, layer and regularisation re-chosen each time, on labels shuffled within each topic and stratum cell. B: cos(d_illegal, d_harm) at every layer, 0 to 32, from the factorial mean-difference directions on the 30 training topics, same colours; +1 means the two directions coincide. The shading around each curve is the 95% range the same cosine takes when the legality labels are swapped within each topic and harm cell (50 swaps), so a curve inside its shading is no more aligned with harm than a relabelled contrast would be. The sentence-only curve stays inside its band at every layer but one (layer 9) and runs +0.09 to +0.19 over the last 16 layers. Under the question the curve leaves its band at layer 14 and stays outside; from about layer 17 the band itself opens to roughly ±0.8, so the margin over the band is small there and the sign is what carries. Values at the layers the headline probes chose: +0.09 at layer 25 (sentence only, band −0.28 to +0.26) and +0.70 at layer 15 (sentence + question, band −0.54 to +0.49). All numbers are read from `probeeval_lp_4b_*.json` and `probeeval_lp_4b_prompted_*.json`; the stored cosine is legal-positive and is negated here.

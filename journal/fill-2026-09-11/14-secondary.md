### 1. Markedness check: body, one paragraph, not page 1

Decision: keep, one paragraph in the body's side-checks after the directions table, with the confound in the same breath. It is the one secondary result that says something the main tables do not, and it is Martin's own question (commit 3876c59, "Martin's illegal-vs-not-legal point").

What it answers in his terms. A two-class probe cannot tell "the model has a concept of illegality and legal is its absence" from "the model has a concept of legality". The check uses the 60 plain acts ("You cook pasta") as the place where nothing legal is being said and asks which design rows move away from it. Along the harm direction the answer is clean: harmless rows sit where the plain acts sit and harmful rows are displaced, so harm is a marked pole with harmless as the default. Along the illegality direction it is not clean: the legal twins are already displaced toward illegal, and the illegal rows go further. So "not illegal" (a plain act) and "legal" (a twin describing a rule-adjacent act done lawfully) are not the same place; the direction is graded, three levels not two, and it cannot be read as an illegal pole with legal as its absence. That is the "not legal is not the same as illegal" point seen from the model's side.

Numbers (sentence only, layer 25, `markedness_lp_4b.json` `summary.illegality_direction.at_chosen_layer`; distances in plain-act standard deviations, held-out topics): illegal-harmful +3.76, illegal-harmless +2.99, legal-harmful +0.91, legal-harmless +1.83. Harm direction (`summary.harm_direction.at_chosen_layer`): +3.45, +0.83, +3.86, +0.09. Illegal rows average +3.37, legal rows +1.37, so illegal rows sit about two and a half times as far out, not twice. Legal-harmless twins +1.83, illegal-harmless +2.99: the twins carry more than half of the plain-act-to-illegal displacement. Plain-act spread along the two directions 1.69 and 1.89 (refit).

Paste-ready:

> One side check, run once and not part of the argument. A two-class probe cannot tell me whether the model's concept is "illegal", with legal as its absence, or "legal". So I projected every held-out sentence onto the illegality and harm directions from the training topics and measured how far each quadrant sits from the 60 plain acts ("You cook pasta"), in units of the plain acts' own spread. Along the harm direction the picture is clean: harmless rows sit where the plain acts sit (+0.1 and +0.8) and harmful rows are displaced (+3.5 and +3.9). Along the illegality direction it is not: the legal twins are already displaced toward illegal (+1.8 legal-harmless, +0.9 legal-harmful) and the illegal rows go further (+3.0, +3.8). So along this direction a lawful act described in the register of the dataset does not sit where a plain act sits; "not illegal" and "legal" are different places, and the ordering is graded rather than a marked illegal pole. I read this as the direction partly encoding that a legal question is in play at all, with illegality on top, and I have not tested that reading. The plain acts are also shorter (7.7 words against 11 to 14), so length or register could produce the shared offset; there is no null behind these numbers and I have not recomputed them by hand. Sentence only, layer 25; the same check at the answer position is not usable because the plain acts' spread there is 0.13, which turns every distance into a large number.

### 2. Illegality direction predicting harm: body, one sentence under the directions table

Decision: keep in the body's directions table where it already is (`journal/results.md`, last column), add one sentence. Not page 1. Its value is that it is the held-out version of the cosine: the cosine is computed on training topics, this AUROC scores the training-topic direction on the 15 test topics' rows.

Numbers (1 − `factorial.dlegal_auroc_predicting_harm_test`): sentence only 0.498 → 0.50 (`probeeval_lp_4b_L_h2nh.json`, layer 25); sentence + question 0.751 → 0.75 (`probeeval_lp_4b_prompted_L_h2nh.json`, layer 15). For scale, the harm direction itself sorts harmful from harmless on the same rows at 0.958 and 0.926 (`factorial.dharm_auroc_test_all`).

Paste-ready:

> The last column is the same alignment read on held-out topics rather than training topics: the illegality direction, built on the 30 training topics, scored for harmful against harmless on the test rows. So the cosine is not a training-set artefact: the direction that was learned as illegality predicts harm on unseen topics only under the question.

### 4. Negation set: body, two sentences in the checks paragraph; the brief's attribution is wrong

Correction first. 36 of 61 called legal is the illegality probe, harmless → harmful, sentence + question (`probeeval_lp_4b_prompted_L_nh2h.json`, `extra_sets.negated.frac_predicted_legal_1` = 0.5902, layer 26, C 10). The harm probe legal → illegal under the question calls 5 of 61 negations harmful (`probeeval_lp_4b_prompted_H_l2i.json`, `frac_predicted_harmful_1` = 0.0820), which is the expected direction.

Decision: keep the counts in the body's checks paragraph (they are in `journal/results.md` already) and add what the counts hide. Two facts. First, for the headline probe the check is close to vacuous: negations score at −7.90 on average, range −13.2 to −0.6, while the design legal rows average −4.15 (refit), so "61 of 61 called legal" says the negations land far past any legal row, not that the probe reads negation. Under the question they sit where the legal rows sit (−3.08 against −3.01, 58 of 61; refit). Second, the one probe that fails the negations is the reverse illegality probe under the question at layer 26: 25 of 61 called illegal, scores spread from −15 to +20 (refit), and it is not memory of the training topics (12 of 31 negations of training-topic rows called illegal against 13 of 30 held-out; refit). All 61 negations are of illegal-harmless rows (`data/scenarios.csv` `notes`, "negation of sNNN"). This is the same probe whose mass-mean direction disagrees with it by half the scale (0.41 against 0.91, skeleton limitation 13), so it belongs in that limitation, unexplained, and it is one more reason the reverse tests are not quoted as evidence.

### 5. Borderline scores: appendix or omit

Decision: omit from the body; one line in an appendix at most. Reason: the effect is only in the same-stratum held-out rows under the question (median |score| 3.83 clear against 1.84 borderline, n 42 and 19, `borderline_scores_lp_4b_prompted.json` `table`), and it is absent in the cross-stratum rows the claim rests on (2.42 against 2.47 there; sentence only 2.69 against 2.28, n 49 and 11, `borderline_scores_lp_4b.json`); almost no sentence sits within 0.5 of the line in any group (0 to 5%), so the probe is never actually undecided on a borderline row; there is no null; and the flags are not spread across quadrants (all 9 flagged rows among the 30 headline test rows are illegal-harmless, the 15 twins carry none; refit from `acts_lp_4b.npz` and the CSV flags), so the tally of flags among the mistakes (4 of 9 sentence only, 9 of 15 under the question) cannot be read as anything. Under Nanda's "each piece of complexity should be there for a reason" it adds nothing to the claim.

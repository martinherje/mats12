## Paste-ready text: "The two directions"

Two mean-difference directions were computed at every layer from the 30 training topics only. The harm direction is harmful minus harmless, averaged over the legal and the illegal rows; the illegality direction is illegal minus legal, averaged over the harmful and the harmless rows. Because each direction is built inside both strata of the other factor, neither can pick up the other by construction; what they share is what the model puts in both. The cosine between them is the number reported, and the reference for it is a label-swap band: the legality labels are shuffled within each topic and harm cell of the training rows, the illegality direction rebuilt, and its cosine with the real harm direction recorded, 50 times per layer; the band is the 2.5 to 97.5 percentile of those (`scripts/probe_eval.py`, section (a); `n_cos_null` 50 in the JSON args).

With the sentence only, the two directions are close to orthogonal at every layer. At the probe's layer, 25, the cosine is +0.09 against a band of −0.28 to +0.26 (`probeeval_lp_4b_L_h2nh.json`, `factorial.cos_dlegal_dharm` −0.0909; `cosine_curve.null_lo/hi` at index 25, signs flipped). Over layers 17 to 32 the curve runs from +0.09 to +0.19 while the band's upper edge runs from +0.19 to +0.35 (`journal/results.md`, Directions). The curve leaves the band at one layer of 33, layer 9, by 0.004 (+0.113 against +0.109); this is noise, not a result.

With the sentence followed by the question, the picture changes at layer 14. Up to layer 13 the cosine stays inside the band (layer 13: +0.26 against an upper edge of +0.31). At layer 14 it is +0.61 against +0.49, at the probe's layer, 15, it is +0.70 against a band of −0.54 to +0.49, and from layer 17 to 32 it runs from +0.85 to +0.95 (`probeeval_lp_4b_prompted_L_h2nh.json`, `cosine_curve`). The curve is outside the band at 19 of 33 layers, 14 through 32, all on the positive side (`journal/results.md`, Directions). The band itself widens in the same layers: its upper edge runs from +0.72 to +0.92 over layers 17 to 32, so at the answer position a relabelled within-cell contrast also lands close to the harm direction, and "outside the band" there is a statement about a margin of a few hundredths. What carries the claim in that condition is the sign, positive at nineteen consecutive layers, and the probe-layer number, +0.70 against +0.49, where the band is still moderate.

The same picture from the projection side. Scoring the harmless-stratum test rows (15 topics, 30 sentences) by their projection on the illegality direction gives AUROC 0.68 with the sentence only; making the direction orthogonal to the harm direction and scoring again gives 0.68, unchanged (`factorial.dlegal_auroc_within_harmless` 0.6756; `dlegal_minus_harm_top1_auroc_within_harmless` 0.6756). With the question the same numbers are 0.85 and 0.74 (0.8489 and 0.7378): a loss of 0.11, not a collapse. Within the harmful stratum the projection costs nothing in either condition (0.87 to 0.86 sentence only; 1.00 to 1.00 with the question). Read the other way, the illegality direction is no predictor of harm on the test rows with the sentence only (AUROC 0.50; stored `dlegal_auroc_predicting_harm_test` 0.5022, printed as 1 − 0.5022) and a fair one with the question (0.75; stored 0.2489). The harm direction predicts harm within both legality strata in both conditions (0.98 and 0.92 sentence only; 0.93 and 1.00 with the question; `dharm_auroc_within_legal`, `dharm_auroc_within_illegal`).

Two things this does not show. First, the projection removes the between-stratum harm contrast, k = 1, the harm direction itself; it does not remove "harm" from the state. Shah et al. argue the harm subspace is low-rank rather than rank one; k = 2 and 3 were computed (`dlegal_minus_harm_top2/top3_*`) but the extra components are the leading principal directions of the per-topic harm contrasts and mix in topic variance, so only k = 1 is quoted. Second, the projection numbers are for the factorial direction built from all four training quadrants, not for the conditional probe of the main test, so 0.68 and 0.85 are not conditional-generalisation numbers and are not compared with the 0.74.

The word "merge" in the summary describes the two directions under the question, not a mechanism. Nothing in the run says which property of the question produces the alignment. The alternative reading is that at the token before a Yes/No answer nearly every within-cell contrast is squeezed onto one axis, and the widening band from layer 17 is consistent with that. The experiment that would tell the two apart, the same extraction under a neutral yes/no question ("Is the following sentence written in the second person?", `journal/design-questions-legality-probe.md` line 16), was cut on 10 Sep under the one-test rule and was not run. It is the first thing to run next.

## Paste-ready text: "What the geometry figures show"

`scripts/geometry.py` draws every sentence as a point in the plane spanned by the two directions at the probe's layer: x is the projection on the harm direction, y the projection on the illegality direction, both centred on the training mean and scaled by the training rows' spread along that axis. The directions are the same two vectors as above, built from the training topics only. Training and validation topics are drawn faint, the 15 held-out topics solid with an X at each quadrant's mean; the 60 plain acts and the 61 negations, which no probe was trained on, are hollow circles and triangles. The axes are the two directions by construction, so the plane is two-dimensional whatever the model does; what is informative is where the four held-out quadrant means and the two check sets land.

Sentence only, layer 25 (`figures/geometry_lp_4b_L25.png`). The four held-out means sit in four corners: illegal-harmful at (+0.56, +0.52), illegal-harmless at (−0.62, +0.18), legal-harmful at (+0.75, −0.73), legal-harmless at (−0.96, −0.33), in (harm, illegality) units of training spread (`13-followup-baseline-recompute-checks.txt`, "geometry: lp_4b"). Harm separates left from right by 1.2 to 1.7 units; illegality separates bottom from top by 0.5 to 1.25 units, less cleanly, with the two harmless means only 0.5 apart on the illegality axis. The plain acts cluster at the bottom left, harmless and legal; the negations cluster low on the illegality axis but at the middle of the harm axis, around (0, −1.5), so a "You do not …" sentence reads as legal without reading as harmless. Individual held-out points overlap heavily across quadrants; the corners are visible in the means, not in the points.

Sentence + question, layer 15 (`figures/geometry_lp_4b_prompted_L15.png`). The cloud lies along one diagonal. Illegal-harmful sits far up and right at (+1.38, +1.45); the other three means sit together at the lower left: illegal-harmless (−0.63, −0.44), legal-harmful (−0.12, −0.64), legal-harmless (−1.10, −0.92) (same source, "geometry: lp_4b_prompted"). The two off-diagonal quadrants, where law and harm disagree, are 0.5 apart on the harm axis and 0.2 apart on the illegality axis, and both sit between the two easy corners on both axes. The plain acts and the negations lie on the same diagonal at its lower end. This is the +0.70 cosine seen as a picture: at the token before the answer one axis carries most of what both directions measure, and the three quadrants that are not illegal-and-harmful are hard to tell apart on either.

What the pictures are and are not. They are the same numbers as the directions table, plotted; they add no new test and no null. The training/validation points are shown to give the reader the shape of the whole set, but only the held-out means are out of sample. The figures were produced once in the 10 Sep follow-up and have not been independently recomputed (`journal/verification-log.md`, "not done" row).

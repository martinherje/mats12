## What is extracted (`scripts/extract_activations.py`)

## The two conditions

- Condition 2 "sentence + question" (run `lp_4b_prompted`): `--template chat --generation-prompt --enable-thinking off --question legal`. The sentence is placed inside the legality question as a single user turn; `add_generation_prompt=True` appends the assistant opener; `enable_thinking=False`. Token position = the final `\n\n` token after the empty `<think></think>` block, i.e. the position the Yes/No answer is generated from (43 tokens for the pasta example).
- Exact question string (`common.py QUESTIONS["legal"]`, `JURISDICTION = "the United States"`):

## Probe (`scripts/probe_eval.py`)
- Classifier: `make_pipeline(StandardScaler(), LogisticRegression(C=C, max_iter=3000))` on the 2560-d residual vector at one layer.
- C grid: 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10 (`--C-grid` default). Layers searched: `range(1, 33)` = hidden-state indices 1–32 (`--layers all` excludes index 0, the embedding).
- Topic split: 60 topics shuffled with `np.random.default_rng(seed=0)`; first 15 = test, next 15 = validation, remaining 30 = train. Same split (seed 0) in all eight designs and both conditions; test topics listed in each JSON `split`.
- Training set: train topics ∩ training stratum (e.g. for L_h2nh: the harmful rows of 30 topics, n = 61 after exclusions; `13-followup…txt` "train n=61").
- Selection: for every (layer, C) fit on train, score AUROC on the validation topics' OTHER stratum (`va_out = is_val & ~in_train_stratum`); pick the max (`select_and_test`). Refit at the chosen (layer, C); test rows scored once.
- Test rows: 15 test topics × 2 rows of the other stratum = 30 sentences (`te_out`); in-stratum test accuracy (`te_in`) also reported. Reported: `test_cross_auroc` (headline), `test_cross_acc` at the fitted cut-off, `test_in_acc`.
- Four designs (`common.DESIGNS`): `L_h2nh` illegality trained harmful → tested harmless (headline); `L_nh2h` reverse; `H_i2l` harm trained illegal → tested legal; `H_l2i` reverse. Each run in both conditions → 8 JSONs.
- Chosen (layer, C) per JSON: bare L_h2nh L25 C1; prompted L_h2nh L15 C0.01; bare L_nh2h L32 C0.01; prompted L_nh2h L26 C10; bare H_i2l L14 C0.01; prompted H_i2l L15 C10; bare H_l2i L3 C10; prompted H_l2i L14 C0.1.
- Topic-block bootstrap: `--n-boot 2000`; resample the 15 test topics with replacement, recompute accuracy → 95% interval from quantiles 0.025/0.975 (`test_cross_acc_ci95`). Interval is on accuracy, not AUROC.
- Permutation null: `--n-perm 100`; labels permuted within each topic × other-factor cell (`shuffle_within_cells`: for each topic and each stratum value, permute the target labels among rows in that cell), then the WHOLE procedure re-run (layer and C re-selected on validation, test scored). Reports null mean/p95/max on accuracy and AUROC, `shuffles_beaten_auroc` and `shuffles_beaten_acc` (count of null runs strictly below the real score), and `p_value_vs_null = (#null_acc ≥ real + 1)/(n+1)`. The docstring says report "beat N of M", not a p-value; the pre-registered count is the AUROC one (results_table.py).
- Factorial directions (on training topics, at each layer): `d_legal = ½[(mean LH − mean IH) + (mean LH̄ − mean IH̄)]`, unit-normalised; `d_harm` likewise with the roles swapped (`factorial_dir`). Uses all four quadrants of the 30 train topics, not the training stratum alone.
- Cosine curve: `cos(d_legal, d_harm)` at every one of the 33 layers (0–32), on training topics. Null band: `--n-cos-null 50` repeats; legal labels swapped within each topic × harm cell on the training topics, `d_legal` recomputed, cosine with the REAL `d_harm`; band = 2.5%/97.5% quantiles. Stored legal-positive; printed/plotted as `cos(d_illegal, d_harm) = −stored`, and the band's ends swap.
- Direction AUROCs at the chosen layer (test topics only): projection `acts @ d` scored by AUROC within each stratum of the other factor (`dlegal_auroc_within_harmful`, `…_within_harmless`, `dharm_auroc_within_legal`, `…_within_illegal`), plus all-quadrant AUROCs and `dlegal_auroc_predicting_harm_test` (printed as 1 − stored).
- Harm projection: harm contrast vectors per training topic within each legality stratum, centred, SVD → principal components. For k = 1, 2, 3: orthonormal basis Q of [d_harm, top k−1 PCs]; `d_legal_resid = d_legal − Q Qᵀ d_legal`, renormalised; AUROC within harmful / within harmless / all test rows. k=1 projects out d_harm itself; the table reports k=1 only.
- Half-contrast (mass-mean) check: direction = mean(target=1) − mean(target=0) in the training stratum only, scored on cross-stratum and in-stratum test rows (`massmean_half_contrast_auroc_*`).
- Extra sets: at the chosen layer, the fitted probe `predict`s the 60 plain acts and 61 negations; JSON stores `frac_predicted_legal_1` (expected all 1 for legality probes) / `frac_predicted_harmful_1` (expected all 0 for harm probes).
- Length-only AUROC: word count (`text.split()` length) as the score on the same 30 cross-stratum test rows, for the label the JSON is written in (legal- or harmful-positive).
- Optional flags not used in the run of record: `--drop-borderline` (uses `borderline_legal` / `borderline_harm`), `--drop-cue-rows` (regex of legality vocabulary; used only for `L_h2nh_nocue`, 241 rows).
- Outputs: `data/processed/probeeval_<run>_<tag>.json`, `factorial_dirs_<run>_<tag>.npz` (d_legal, d_harm, d_legal_resid, layer), `figures/probeeval_<run>_<tag>.png`.

## 4. The three sentences most likely to lose points, and the fix

- **"Qwen3.5-4B has a linear representation of legality."** Loses on his named common mistake ("showing that a safety-related concept has a linear representation") and on "Make plausible claims over ambitious ones"; with 30 test rows and a 53–83% accuracy interval it is also unsupported. Fix: "An illegality probe trained inside the harmful stratum ranks harmless sentences of unseen topics at 0.74, on thirty sentences. That is what the bare result licenses; whether it is a legality representation or a residue of graded harm (the harm probe sorts the same stratum at 0.61) is open." Lead with the dissociation test and the condition contrast, never with "we found a legality probe".
- **"The model knows more than it says."** Loses on "Trying to hype up their results ... Just be honest! I can tell", and it smuggles a claim about knowledge from a two-token argmax. Fix, in the numbers' own terms: "The model's graded Yes−No logit ranks illegal above legal within each stratum (0.83 harmless, 0.95 harmful); its argmax answer says 'illegal' only when the act is also harmful (34% of illegal-harmless rows, 25% of legal-harmful). The distinction is present in the score and absent from the one-word answer, under one question wording, on one model." No "knows", no "hides", no "discarded" as a verb of intent.
- **"This is the first legality probe."** Loses on "Originality is a big plus" read the wrong way: he checks, Sadhu and Schwarz exist, and a priority claim invites him to find the counterexample. Fix: drop "first" entirely, or the run-sheet form at most once in the body ("first topic-matched illegality × harm test"). On page 1 say what it adds in one clause: the illegality × harm factorial with conditional generalisation across strata on unseen topics, the angle between the factorial directions against a label-swap null, and the model's own answer as the baseline.
- Three more that cost points quietly: "cos 0.9, above the null band" (the band there is ±0.8; quote the probe-layer +0.70 against −0.54 to +0.49); "the two conditions give the same AUROC" (167 of 225 rank pairs in both, a coincidence; say "also 0.74"); "from layer 13" (layer 13 is +0.26 inside a band whose top is +0.31; it is layer 14).

## 5. Presenting the negative and inconclusive parts as strengths

- His sentence to write toward: "Negative or inconclusive results that are well-analysed are much better than a poorly supported positive result." And: "The difference between 'I got stuck so I gave up' and 'I got stuck, so I pivoted or found a new angle, or identified the reason why it didn't work' is huge."
- **The probe loses to asking (0.81 vs 0.74).** Present as the answer to the monitoring question, which is the pragmatic-interp question he says he cares about ("measured against baselines"): on this model a bare-sentence illegality probe adds nothing over the model's own logit, so the interest is in what the geometry shows about how the model computes the answer, not in the probe as a tool. Say "the probe does not beat asking" in those words; do not soften to "comparable".
- **27 of 60 plain acts called illegal.** Present as the distinction between ranking and classifying: the probe ranks held-out rows above the null and its cut-off does not survive off-distribution. Name the length hypothesis (7.7 vs 11–14 words) and say it is untested. His register: "I do not have an explanation for this and I would not build a monitor on it."
- **Prompted accuracy 50%, every row called legal.** Same shape: ranking survives (0.74, 100 of 100 on AUROC), cut-off dies (36 of 100 on accuracy). This is also the stated reason bare is presented first even though prompted was pre-registered as main; saying so turns a pre-hoc/post-hoc liability into a documented decision.
- **The neutral-question control was cut.** Present as the one experiment that separates the two readings of the prompted result (a legality-specific merge vs. a collapse of every within-cell contrast onto the answer axis), name both readings, say it was cut for time, and put it first under "what next". He asks for exactly this: "which parts are speculative, what you would investigate next."
- **Predicted 0.85, got 0.74.** The miss is the evidence that the prediction was pre-hoc. Quote the numbers as written on 10 Sep, do not explain the gap away.
- **The reverse test undercut by word count (0.79 vs 0.71).** Say it once, do not quote 0.71 as evidence anywhere; a confound found and declared reads as attention to detail ("Noticing subtleties and edge cases").
- **The harmless stratum is not harm-flat (0.61 / 0.74, agent).** This is the strongest threat to the claim and belongs first in the evidence-against paragraph precisely because he will think of it; finding it already there is his named "really positive sign".
- **The 2×2 dataset was rewritten twice.** Disclosed as two confounds found mid-project and fixed (cue words after the pilot's asymmetric transfer; length after word count hit 0.96), with the numbers before and after. That is the "pivoted or found a new angle" story, told in two clauses.
- **Length of the not-concluded list.** Two sentences on page 1, the rest as numbered limitations in the body. More than two on page 1 reads as hedging rather than scepticism.

# 06 — Related work facts (legality × harm probe)

Sources: vault lit notes (Sadhu, Schwarz, Bertolazzi, Cho), `scratchpad/review/literature.md` (10 Sep scan, arXiv ids verified against abs pages), `~/mats12/journal/design-questions-legality-probe.md` §"Prior work to cite in the first paragraph". Compiled 2026-09-11.

## (1) Top six — finding, and what this project adds

- **Sadhu, Sengupta, Sankarapu & Seth 2026 (2608.16852).** Finding: cheap compliance detectors (diff-of-means adherence direction; Llama Guard 3, Qwen3Guard) are rule-blind — deleting/permuting the governing rule leaves AUROC unchanged (mean +0.003), a 2×2 rule × scenario benchmark puts every cheap detector at chance (0.50–0.54) while a CoT judge reaches 0.849, and compliance↔safety directions do not transfer (0.552 / 0.476 AUROC); twelve models incl. Qwen3.5-4B (this project's checkpoint — quote their Table 8 numbers when comparing). Adds: Sadhu probes adherence to a *supplied* rule (composition); this project probes standing legality knowledge crossed with harm within one scenario, tests conditional generalisation across harm strata on unseen topics, reports the angle between factorial directions against a null, and steers — not "crossed controls", which they already have (group-disjoint CV, quadruples as unit).
- **Schwarz 2026 (2607.13075).** Finding: residual-stream harm probes are risk-topic detectors, not context adjudicators — 95–98% block rate on compliant attacks but only 0.66–0.82 AUROC on surface-matched benign controls, 80–100% FPR at 95% TPR on XSTest-style prompts; refit-permutation nulls (1,000 medians); PC1 ≈ mean-difference direction at |cos| 0.85–0.94; no legal condition, no Qwen3.5. Adds: the same topic-matched control run on a *legal* axis rather than harm, asking whether any legality signal survives once harm is held fixed or projected out (and reporting what fraction of d_legal's norm remains after removing the harm PCs Schwarz shows dominate).
- **Bertolazzi, Pezzelle & Bernardi 2025 (2510.06700).** Finding: logical validity and plausibility are both linearly represented and aligned (cos 0.48–0.64 at steerable layers, vs specificity comparators cos(validity, harmlessness) 0.10–0.13), plausibility vectors causally bias validity judgements, a debiasing vector (μ_valid − μ_plausible) reduces the content effect; 1,280 syllogisms; Qwen2.5-7B/14B/32B, Qwen3-4B/14B/32B, Gemma-3; evaluated on the same test split. Adds: same recipe, different concept pair — a normative concept (legality) crossed with a correlated content concept (harm) whose off-diagonal cells exist in the world; held-out cross-stratum generalisation on unseen topics (they have none); label-swap null on the cosine over layers; 240 topic-matched hand-checked quadruples rather than 1,280 templated syllogisms (state this, a reviewer will ask).
- **Cho, Li & Leshinskaya 2026 (2602.19101, v2 Jun 2026).** Finding: *value entanglement* — moral, grammatical and economic value probed by mean-difference vectors on factorial stimuli in Qwen2.5-7B, Gemma-2/3, Qwen3-8B/14B/32B; moral and grammatical projections correlate up to r = 0.75; directional ablation of the moral vector repairs grammatical judgement (r with ground truth .20 → .55); no cross-generalisation test, no cosine null. Adds: legality as the second concept; conditional generalisation across strata on unseen topics; cosine reported against a label-swap null; steering the *target* (legality) judgement rather than ablating the contaminant. Cite in the first paragraph as the closest conceptual sibling.
- **Zhao et al. 2025 (2507.11878, NeurIPS 2025).** Finding: harmfulness and refusal are encoded separately — diff-in-means at t_inst vs t_post-inst on Llama2-7B-Chat, Llama3-8B-Instruct, Qwen2-7B-Instruct; cos ≈ 0.1 with no null; steering along harm flips the harm judgement, steering along refusal does not. Adds: the steering-dissociation logic applied to a third axis (legality is not a relabelling of harm or refusal), and a null band on the cosine that Zhao lacks.
- **Xu, Rusnak & Kaplan 2026 (2603.23659).** Finding: probes for five ethical frameworks across six LLMs (4B–72B) occupy differentiated subspaces with asymmetric transfer (deontology → virtue partly generalises; commonsense → justice fails); post-hoc validation shows probes partly read benchmark template surface features. Adds: a two-factor design that holds the confound fixed while generalising (rather than transferring across subtypes), a zero-cue rewrite and hand-check at dataset construction to pre-empt the template confound; asymmetric transfer stays a footnote here because Xu already reports it.

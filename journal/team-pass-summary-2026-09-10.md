## 1. The finding, as it should now be stated

An illegality probe trained on harmful sentences only still sorts illegal from legal among harmless sentences of fifteen unseen topics (AUROC 0.74, above all 100 shuffled-label runs, in both conditions), and while the model reads the bare sentence the illegality and harm directions are unaligned (cos +0.09 inside a band of about ±0.3, harm projected out 0.68 → 0.68), but once it is asked "is this illegal?" they line up (cos +0.70 at the probe's layer against a band of −0.54 to +0.49, outside the band from layer 14, harm projected out 0.85 → 0.74, the probe's cut-off calling every harmless test row legal).

Martin's reading stands: the model keeps illegality and harm apart while it reads and merges them as it prepares a yes/no. Three corrections to how it was written on the run sheet at 20:30: the curve leaves the band at layer 14, not 13 (layer 13 is +0.26 inside a band whose top is +0.31); the projection number is 0.85 → 0.74 at k = 1, not 0.64 (that was k = 2, which removes topic-variance components, not harm); and "merge" is not to be written as a mechanism, because from layer 17 the prompted band itself is ±0.8 to 0.9 and the neutral-question control that would tell a legality-specific merge from a general collapse onto the answer axis was cut.
